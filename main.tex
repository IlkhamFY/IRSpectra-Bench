\begin{abstract}
Frontier models have shown strong performance on molecular structure elucidation,
but it is unclear how well those results transfer to heterogeneous,
experimentally reported spectral data.
We ask an operational question: given only a molecular formula and
\textbf{literature-reported} IR, $^1$H and $^{13}$C NMR \textbf{peak lists} ---
not digitised spectral images --- can an off-the-shelf frontier model recover
the correct constitution, and \textbf{which stage fails} when it cannot?

We release \textbf{IRSpectra-Bench}, a blind peak-list benchmark of
\textbf{$n{=}500$} compounds drawn from the companion \textbf{IRexp}
resource~\citep{yabbarov2026irexp}, with a fixed RDKit InChIKey-connectivity
contract and decomposable \textbf{generation recall} and
\textbf{verification precision} metrics.
A frontier model recovers \textbf{45.4\%} top-1 (227/500) and \textbf{49.8\%}
generation recall (249/500).
When the true structure is proposed, self-ranking places it first in
$227/249$ cases (91.2\%).
Independent forward verification of the same pools yields the $n{=}500$ wall of
\textbf{204} verified / \textbf{45} misranked / \textbf{251} never proposed:
proposal, not ranking, is the expensive stage
(\textbf{propose $\gg$ verify}).
The same recall $\ll$ precision asymmetry replicates across
\textbf{four vendor families}.
Formula-only controls collapse; accuracy is flat in source-paper year;
broader generation sampling lifts recall, whereas verification alone barely
moves top-1.
From published top-$k$ figures, reduced recall accounts for
\textbf{68--83\%} of the accuracy collapse from curated or simulated spectra
to real heterogeneous peak lists.
Frozen predictions, scorers and code are released for mechanical re-evaluation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Structure elucidation from spectra sits at the centre of synthetic and analytical chemistry.
Infrared and one-dimensional NMR are the first-line measurements a bench chemist
records; papers report them as printed peak lists, not digitised absorbance traces
or FIDs. Learned systems now consume those same reports as sequence or agent tasks.
Encoder--decoder models train on paired corpora~\citep{chacko2024spectro,ottomano2025nmiracle,alberts2024ir};
LLM agents and puzzle benches report high recovery on curated or education
spectra~\citep{kamber2026chemist,guo2024molpuzzle,zhuang2025treesearch,espejo2026agentic}.
Reported recovery for the same model can invert with the harness
(GPT-4o, 1.4\% vs.\ 57.8\% on MolPuzzle~\citep{guo2024molpuzzle,zhuang2025treesearch}).
The operational questions are whether an off-the-shelf model can
\textbf{recover constitution from peak lists as printed in open-access papers},
under a fixed molecular-representation scoring contract, and
\textbf{which stage binds} when it cannot --- proposal versus verification.

End-to-end top-1 is the wrong reporting primitive.
Any system that returns a ranked candidate list factorises as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:factor}
\text{top-1} \;=\; \underbrace{P(\text{true} \in \text{pool})}_{\text{generation recall}}
\times
\underbrace{P(\text{rank}=1 \mid \text{true} \in \text{pool})}_{\text{verification precision}}.
\end{equation}
Recall is a fraction of $n$ problems; precision $|$ recall is a fraction of the
recalled subset. The two rates have different denominators and must not be
differenced (Eqs.~\ref{eq:top1}--\ref{eq:prec}).
Prior work already notes that re-ranking cannot recover a missing
candidate~\citep{priessner2026reasoning}; we report both factors on a frozen
literature-peak-list roster at $n{=}500$.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{IRSpectra-Bench} --- a blind, complexity-stratified peak-list benchmark ($n{=}500$)
        with a pre-registered InChIKey-connectivity scorer and community reporting contract
        (top-1, generation recall, verification precision $|$ recall).
  \item \textbf{A recall-bound diagnosis} on a frontier LLM: propose $\gg$ verify.
        Generation recall $249/500$ (49.8\%); self-ranking precision $|$ recall
        $227/249$ (91.2\%); forward-verify wall $204$ verified / $45$ misranked /
        $251$ never proposed (Figure~\ref{fig:fig1}).
  \item \textbf{Contamination and cross-vendor controls.} Formula-only and recency
        bounds; the recall $\ll$ precision gap replicates in four vendor families.
  \item \textbf{A literature decomposition} that recovers the same split from published top-$k$
        figures, showing recall carries most of the collapse on real heterogeneous data.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Dataset pointer.}
Peak lists come from the companion \textbf{IRexp} resource (121,233 records; 43,060
structure-linked; 33,201 full IR+$^1$H+$^{13}$C+structure quadruples); an anonymised
review copy is in Appendix~\ref{app:dataset}.
Corpus construction and licensing are the subject of a \textit{Scientific Data}
manuscript (in preparation)~\citep{yabbarov2026irexp}, not of this paper.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Trained spectra $\to$ structure models}
Sequence and graph decoders on paired
corpora~\citep{chacko2024spectro,hu2024multitask,yang2026nmrtrans,ottomano2025nmiracle,alberts2024ir,alberts2025benchmarks}
are accurate in-distribution but typically scored end-to-end.
Spectro, NMIRacle, Alberts IR transformers and CASE are \textbf{not scored} on
this bench (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}); the released scorer is the intended
comparison route.

\subsection{Agentic elucidation: three regimes}
The contrast is the \textbf{setting and the stage split}.
(i)~\emph{Tool-using lab agents} --- ChemCrow~\citep{mbran2024chemcrow},
Coscientist~\citep{boiko2023coscientist} --- call instruments; they do not measure
which elucidation stage binds.
(ii)~\emph{Spectrum-native agents} re-rank a \textbf{fixed} pool:
tree-search~\citep{zhuang2025treesearch}, SpecMol~\citep{shen2025specmol},
Priessner \textit{et al.}~\citep{priessner2026reasoning},
NMR-Solver~\citep{jin2025nmrsolver}, NMRAgent~\citep{fang2026nmragent}.
Priessner already notes that re-ranking cannot recover a missing candidate; we measure
that failure on literature peak lists at $n{=}500$.
SpectraLLM~\citep{su2025spectrallm} is a \textbf{fine-tuned} elucidator on
\textbf{simulated} corpora --- a different object; we do not score it here.
IR-Agent~\citep{noh2025iragent} (ICLR 2026) is the closest concurrent IR elucidator
(NIST experimental IR; TI+Ret features + SE re-rank over a proposed pool).
Its SE expert is a verifier over a proposed pool: it can change which candidate is
ranked first, not whether the true structure was proposed.
TI+Ret features likewise cannot repair a missing candidate.
(iii)~\emph{Agentic-search benches} --- MolQuest~\citep{han2026molquest},
NMRGym~\citep{fang2026nmrgym}, Espejo Morales \textit{et al.}~\citep{espejo2026agentic}
(education $\to$ industrial: 80.9\% $\to$ 20.6\%) --- ask which architecture wins.
IRSpectra-Bench instead reports generation recall versus verification
precision $|$ recall for an off-the-shelf closed-book model on literature peak lists
(Eqs.~\ref{eq:top1}--\ref{eq:prec}).
We do not claim to beat SpectraLLM or IR-Agent on their native sets.

\subsection{Heterogeneous data, CASE, and positioning}
Most prior suites use simulated or single-instrument spectra; we use literature-reported
peak lists drawn from many laboratories and report formula-only and recency controls
(Section~\ref{sec:contamination}).
Matching predicted to observed shifts --- DP4~\citep{smith2010dp4},
CASE~\citep{elyashberg2012case}, NMR-Solver, NMRAgent --- is a verification problem;
CASE enumerators have exhaustive recall by construction, while published LLM
elucidation numbers rarely state whether the true structure was proposed at all.
SpecX~\citep{xiang2026specx} bounds a related multi-modal spectroscopy task on
simulated traces.
IRSpectra-Bench is an openly redistributable literature-peak-list suite with a
\textbf{stage decomposition}, measured on Claude and replicated across three other
model families.

\section{IRSpectra-Bench}
\label{sec:benchmark}

\subsection{Task and inputs}

Each of 500 problems supplies the \textbf{molecular formula} (as from HRMS), the \textbf{IR
band list} (cm$^{-1}$), and \textbf{$^1$H / $^{13}$C shift lists} --- exactly the textual form
reported in open-access papers.
No name, SMILES or scaffold hint.
Inputs are author-transcribed \textbf{band and shift lists}, not absorbance traces or FIDs:
a different object from digitised-spectrum benchmarks~\citep{espejo2026agentic}, and the
form language models read from the literature.

The evaluation set is a frozen roster of 500 question ids released with the code.
Problems are drawn from the structure-complete split of IRexp (\texttt{irexp\_resolved})~\citep{yabbarov2026irexp}.
Figure~\ref{fig:fig-chemspace} summarises the chemistry space.
Listing~\ref{lst:payload} is the solver-facing contract: formula plus printed peak lists;
gold constitution is held out and scored offline.

\subsection{Difficulty and scoring}

\textbf{Difficulty} is declared \emph{a priori} by RDKit ring analysis: \emph{simple} iff
$\leq$2 rings, no fused/spiro/bridgehead system, $\leq$22 heavy atoms; else \emph{complex}
(248/252 on $n{=}500$).
The released benchmark is \textbf{near-50/50 by design};
the eligible corpus is 17.5\% simple / 82.5\% complex, so the leaderboard is not a
draw from the corpus mix.
Reweighted to corpus composition on a validate-clean subset, top-1 falls to
\textbf{26.5\%} [21--32] and recall to \textbf{31.3\%} [25--37]
(SI; not an $n{=}500$ figure).
Headline scores in Table~\ref{tab:headline} are the 50/50 leaderboard.

\textbf{Scoring.} A candidate $\hat y$ is correct at constitution iff the first 14
characters of the RDKit InChIKey match
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ik14}
s(\hat y, y^\star)
\;=\;
\mathbf{1}\!\big[\mathrm{IK}_{14}(\hat y)=\mathrm{IK}_{14}(y^\star)\big].
\end{equation}
Full stereo-sensitive InChIKey is reported on the validate-clean subset
(SI).
External submissions: up to three ranked SMILES per \texttt{qid}, no structure hints
(\texttt{docs/LEADERBOARD.md}; \texttt{scripts/score\_submission.py}).
With a ranked pool $\mathcal{C}_i=(c_{i1},\ldots,c_{ik})$, $k\leq 3$,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:top1}
\mathrm{top\text{-}1}
&\;=\;
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n s(c_{i1}, y_i^\star),\\
\label{eq:recall}
\mathrm{recall}@k
&\;=\;
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbf{1}\!\big[\exists\,j\leq k:\; s(c_{ij}, y_i^\star)=1\big],\\
\label{eq:prec}
\mathrm{prec.}|\mathrm{recall}
&\;=\;
\frac{\sum_i s(c_{i1}, y_i^\star)}
{\sum_i \mathbf{1}[y_i^\star\in\mathcal{C}_i]}.
\end{align}
Top-1 and recall@$k$ share the denominator $n$; precision $|$ recall is defined
only on the recalled subset.
Eq.~\ref{eq:factor} is exactly $\mathrm{top\text{-}1}=(\mathrm{recall}@k)\times(\mathrm{prec.}|\mathrm{recall})$
when both stages use the same ranked list: a better ranker cannot raise recall.
Confidence intervals are bootstrap 95\%; paired model differences use McNemar with Holm correction.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Blind payload and solver.}
The solver sees four JSON fields per \texttt{qid}: \texttt{formula} (as from HRMS),
\texttt{ir\_bands\_cm-1}, \texttt{h\_nmr}, \texttt{c\_nmr} --- peak lists as printed
(Listing~\ref{lst:payload}).
No name, SMILES, scaffold, atom-type hint, or retrieved spectrum enters the prompt;
ground truth is scored offline.
The headline solver is Claude Opus via a consumer subscription, closed-book, with
one sub-agent per batch and an RDKit formula check only.
The core protocol uses no fine-tuning, no paid API, and \textbf{no thinking tier}.
Inference is \textbf{not} exactly reproducible (no pinned snapshot, temperature or seed ---
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
\textbf{Scoring is reproducible}: frozen predictions, ground truth and scorers regenerate every
training-free number.

\paragraph{Scoring contract and candidate budget.}
A deposit is JSONL of \texttt{\{qid, candidates: [SMILES,\ldots]\}} under a stated budget.
The scorer reports the three Section~\ref{sec:benchmark} metrics at InChIKey-connectivity,
with bootstrap 95\% CIs.
Self-ranking and forward-verified re-ranking must be labelled separately; they are not
interchangeable.
Headline protocol: $\leq$3 ranked SMILES per \texttt{qid}.
On the 60-compound arm Claude returned mean 2.20 candidates vs 3.00 for the other vendors
(recall rankings are therefore approximate).
\textbf{Generate-wide} (that arm only): ten independent solvers $\times$ up to six SMILES,
pooled then re-ranked --- not the $n{=}500$ reporting budget.

\paragraph{Forward-verification and probes.}
Generate $\to$ forward-predict $^{13}$C (blind to the observed list) $\to$ re-rank by
symmetric chamfer on $^{13}$C peak sets $P,Q\subset\mathbb{R}$
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:chamfer}
d(P,Q)
\;=\;
\frac1{|P|}\sum_{p\in P}\min_{q\in Q}|p-q|
\;+\;
\frac1{|Q|}\sum_{q\in Q}\min_{p\in P}|q-p|
\end{equation}
(Section~\ref{sec:forward-verify}).
This second ranker uses the same candidate pool; it cannot raise generation recall.
Controls: formula-only / recency (Section~\ref{sec:contamination}); four-vendor replication
on a 60-compound arm (Section~\ref{sec:cross-vendor}); generate-wide sampling; HOSE lookup
and GNN $^{13}$C verifiers; a small IRexp-fine-tuned generator probe (excluded from headline
claims).

\subsection{Cross-vendor replication}
\label{sec:cross-vendor}

Grok 4.6, Gemini 3.7 Flash and GPT-5.6 Sol solved the same 60-compound arm under the
identical protocol.
\textbf{Verification precision exceeds generation recall in every arm}; bootstrapping
separates the paired gap for Claude (+52.5 points), GPT-5.6 Sol (+26.3) and Gemini (+23.3);
Grok's gap (+9.2) is directional.
Candidate budgets differ (Claude mean 2.20 vs others 3.00), so recall rankings are
approximate.
A clean-clone control for Grok bounds key leakage (McNemar $p{=}0.39$).

The $n{=}500$ forward-verify wall is $204$ verified / $45$ misranked / $251$ never
proposed.
No re-ranking repairs the $251$ compounds where the true structure was never
proposed (Figure~\ref{fig:fig1}).

\paragraph{Worked cases.}
Three deposits instantiate the wall (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:cases}).
\textbf{main-R06} (C$_{29}$H$_{30}$FNO$_{2}$): true acridinedione never enters the 3-candidate
pool (Tanimoto-0.90 regioisomer); verifier unused.
\textbf{v3-R25} (C$_{10}$H$_{14}$N$_{2}$O): verify-save --- self-rank prefers 3-pyridyl,
forward $^{13}$C recovers 2-pyridyl (chamfer 0.42 vs 1.30~ppm; Figure~\ref{fig:fig-mechanism}).
\textbf{v3-R26} (C$_{14}$H$_{11}$N$_{3}$O$_{3}$): verify-fail --- true 3-nitrophenyl isomer in
the pool but 2-nitro ranked first (chamfer 1.35 vs 1.36~ppm).

\paragraph{Generate-wide.}
Ten solver agents, up to six candidates each, pooled and re-ranked on the 60-compound arm:
recall 32\% $\to$ \textbf{42\%}, top-1 23\% $\to$ \textbf{30\%} (McNemar $p{=}0.34$);
verification precision falls 84\% $\to$ 72\%.
The training-free ceiling remains recall-limited.

\paragraph{Non-LLM verifiers.}
On the same candidate sets, a HOSE-style lookup ties self-ranking; a modest GNN reaches
59/65 (91\%) vs the LLM verifier's 58/65 (89\%) --- directional.
Deranging observed $^{13}$C pairings collapses precision ($p{=}0.001$).
A small IRexp-fine-tuned generator pooled with Claude lifts recall on an
instrumented slice $33.5\%\to54.1\%$ and top-1 $28.4\%\to35.1\%$
(McNemar $p{=}0.015$), showing the wall is a property of
\textbf{training-free elicitation}, not of the task.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Frontier LLMs are \textbf{good verifiers} ($227/249$ self-rank on $n{=}500$) and
\textbf{weak proposers} (50\% recall on $n{=}500$) --- propose $\gg$ verify.
The contribution is the \textbf{benchmark + stage decomposition} on the frozen
$n{=}500$ roster, not a solved elucidator and not an IRexp Data Descriptor.

\paragraph{Reporting contract.}
External work should deposit ranked SMILES and report (i)~top-1 constitution
(Eq.~\ref{eq:top1}), (ii)~generation recall (Eq.~\ref{eq:recall}), (iii)~verification
precision $|$ recall (Eq.~\ref{eq:prec}), with bootstrap CIs and candidate budget.
Nonsignificant ladder gains (forward-verification vs self-ranking, $p{=}0.55$;
generate-wide top-1, $p{=}0.34$) are \textbf{diagnostic}, not accuracy advances.
Corpus-reweighted top-1 (\textbf{26.5\%} on a validate-clean subset) remains the
better estimate for an arbitrary literature draw; it was not re-scored as $n{=}500$.
IRexp fine-tuning already lifts recall on an instrumented slice $33.5\%\to54.1\%$.

\paragraph{Implications for agentic elucidation.}
\label{sec:agentic}
Proposal is the expensive stage: diversity, enumeration / database search,
formula-constrained decode, or generate-wide sampling that enlarges the pool.
Verification is cheap: once proposed, self-ranking selects the
true constitution $227/249$ times on $n{=}500$.
A second ranker cannot recover the $251$ never proposed.
IR-Agent-style TI+Ret~\citep{noh2025iragent} are complementary \emph{feature} agents;
MolQuest- and Espejo-style architecture search~\citep{han2026molquest,espejo2026agentic}
still needs this stage contract.
The binding improvement is a better proposer, not a second ranker.
Nonsignificant ladder steps do not license a solved-elucidator headline.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\label{sec:limitations}
Scoring is mechanical; solver runs were transcript-audited for zero ground-truth access.
\textbf{(i) Consumer harness.} Claude Opus via consumer subscription; no snapshot,
temperature, seed, or thinking tier. Inference is not bit-exact; scoring is.
Batch instruction text was not captured.
Higher-reasoning inference modes are not treated as a scored method result.
\textbf{(ii) Contamination} is bounded by formula-only ($23\%\to5\%$) and flat recency
but \textbf{not excluded}.
\textbf{(iii) Object / formula / stereo.} Band lists, not traces; formula supplied
(as from HRMS). Stereo top-1 on a validate-clean subset is in the SI.
\textbf{(iv) Statistics.} Forward-verify vs self-rank unresolved ($p{=}0.55$);
generate-wide top-1 likewise ($p{=}0.34$). The $n{=}24$ four-model arm is underpowered.
The ladder \textbf{diagnoses a bottleneck}; it is not an accuracy advance.
\textbf{(v) Missing on-bench baselines.} SpectraLLM, IR-Agent, Spectro, NMIRacle, Alberts IR
transformers and CASE are \textbf{not scored} here.
\textbf{(vi) Forward-verify coverage.} The $n{=}500$ wall is
$204/45/251$ (Figure~\ref{fig:fig1}; reprint Figure~\ref{fig:fig-wall}).
Protocol-slice and expand-500 chamfer arms remain SI, not a second wall.
\textbf{(vii) Deferred.} Expert-chemist audit not run; leave-one-modality abandoned;
parser audit deferred to the Sci.\ Data companion.
\textbf{(viii) Scope.} Battery subset is literature electrolyte chemistry, not operando;
single-sample scoring; PMC-OA organic bias.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

IRSpectra-Bench makes LLM structure elucidation from literature peak lists \textbf{measurable
and decomposable}.
On the generation cohort ($n{=}500$), a frontier LLM recovers 45.4\% top-1 (227/500)
and 49.8\% generation recall (249/500); corpus-reweighted top-1 on a validate-clean
subset is 26.5\%.
Propose $\gg$ verify: the wall is $204$ verified / $45$ misranked / $251$ never proposed.
The diagnosis replicates across vendors and is recovered from published top-$k$ figures.
We release frozen predictions and a mechanical scorer so others can report the same three
numbers.
The band-list resource behind the bench is documented separately as a
\textit{Scientific Data} Data Descriptor (in preparation)~\citep{yabbarov2026irexp}.

Frontier LLMs were used as \textbf{experimental solvers} under the consumer harness in
Sections~\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:results} (Claude Opus on the headline cohort; three

scoring, or claim-checking.
Inference used no thinking tier and no pinned model snapshot.
We did not use generative AI to invent or alter reported metrics.
Generative tools assisted manuscript editing and formatting; the authors reviewed the text
and take responsibility for all claims, including any AI-assisted wording.

This work evaluates publicly available open-access literature peak lists and commercial LLM
