\begin{abstract}
Frontier models are often presented as near-solved structure elucidators on
curated spectra.
We ask an operational question: given the molecular formula ($f_{\cal M}$) and
literature-reported IR, $^1$H, and $^{13}$C NMR peak lists,
printed wavenumbers and shift tables, not digitised spectra images,
can an out-of-the-box frontier model in an agentic workflow recover the correct constitution, and
which stage fails when it does not?
% Recovery on curated libraries, simulated traces, or single-instrument absorbance is not a measurement of this input.
We release \textbf{IRSpectra-Bench} as a frozen roster of
$500$ problems, an RDKit InChIKey-14 contract, and the
prediction deposits a mechanical scorer can replay.
The object is a factorised diagnosis of one closed-book harness.
One trained NMR-only baseline is scored on the same roster: NMRTrans,
formula and printed $^1$H/$^{13}$C lists, IR unused (79/500 top-1; Appendix~\ref{si:nmrtrans}).
It is not a full multi-modal peer of the IR+$^1$H+$^{13}$C task.
On one closed-book frontier harness, generation top-1 is 45.4\%
(227/500) and recall (the fraction of problems whose true structure enters the proposed pool) is 49.8\% (249/500).
Self-ranking places the true structure first in 91.2\% of the cases ($227/249$).
The generation wall is
227 exact top-1 / 22 in-pool not top-1 / 251 never proposed.
The roster is near-50/50 simple/complex; reweighted to the eligible corpus
mix, top-1 on a validate-clean subset is 26.5\%.
Forward verification of the same pools is a secondary drop, not a second wall:
top-1 falls from 227/500 to 204/500 ($-23$;
diagnostic $204/45/251$; Appendix~\ref{si:fverify}).
On a 60-compound arm only, formula-only recovery is 3/60, accuracy is flat
in source-paper year, and recall stays below precision across four vendor
families.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Predicting the molecular structure of a compound from spectral information, like mid-infrared spectra and one-dimensional NMR chemical shifts, is a routine task in synthetic chemistry, as it validates chemical reaction steps. 
Infrared band positions encode functional groups and conjugation, for example,
carbonyl stretches, alcohols and amines envelopes, aromatic C=C, and the paired
nitro bands that survive even a terse experimental section.
NMR chemical shifts, multiplicities and integrals encode the number and
neighbourhood of hydrogen ($^1$H  NMR) and carbon environments ($^{13}$C NMR).
The form that enters the literature is rarely the instrument file.
Experimental journals commonly report \textbf{peak lists}, wavenumbers in cm$^{-1}$ and
shift tables in ppm, often with coupling constants, and only rarely with
intensities or lineshape, and not digitized spectra. 
Furthermore, 2D correlations (COSY, HSQC, HMBC) that classical
computer-assisted structure elucidation (CASE) uses to constrain
connectivity~\citep{elyashberg2012case,elyashberg2015case} are frequently
absent from those tables.
A model that reads the open literature must therefore invert constitution
from formula plus three printed lists, not from a digitised trace or a
full CASE constraint set.

Learned systems now consume those same reports as sequence or tool-using
tasks.
Encoder--decoder models train on paired spectra--structure
corpora~\citep{chacko2024spectro,ottomano2025nmiracle,alberts2024ir};
puzzle benches and search systems report high recovery on curated/simulated or
education spectra~\citep{kamber2026chemist,guo2024molpuzzle,zhuang2025treesearch,espejo2026agentic}.
Reported recovery for the same model can invert with the harness
(GPT-4o, 1.4\% vs.\ 57.8\% on MolPuzzle~\citep{guo2024molpuzzle,zhuang2025treesearch}),
which already shows that a single end-to-end number is not a stable
property of the chemistry.
The operational setting that matches how chemists read papers is stricter:
an out-of-the-box model, a molecular formula $f_{\cal M}$ (as from high-resolution mass
spectrometry), IR/$^1$H/$^{13}$C lists as printed, a fixed
molecular-representation scoring contract, and a measurement of
\textbf{which stage binds} when recovery fails.
That stage is either proposal of the true constitution or ranking among
candidates that were already proposed.

Any system that returns a ranked molecular candidate list factorizes as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:factor}
\text{top-1} \;=\; \underbrace{P(\text{true} \in \text{pool})}_{\text{generation recall}}
\times
\underbrace{P(\text{rank}=1 \mid \text{true} \in \text{pool})}_{\text{verification precision}}.
\end{equation}
The same split is the classical CASE architecture: generate constitutional
isomers, then match predicted to observed
spectra~\citep{elyashberg2015case,priessner2026reasoning}.
Recall is a fraction of $n$ problems; precision $|$ recall is a fraction of
the recalled subset.
The two rates have different denominators and must not be different
(Eqs.~\ref{eq:metrics}).
A better ranker cannot raise recall, because re-ranking cannot recover a
structure that never entered the
pool~\citep{priessner2026reasoning}.
We report both factors on a frozen literature-peak-list roster at
$n{=}500$, so that a high top-1 cannot hide a missing-candidate failure
and a high verifier score cannot be read as a solved elucidator.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related-work}

% \subsection{Trained spectra $\to$ structure models}
Paired corpora have produced strong in-distribution elucidators, and they
set the default reporting language for the field. For example, \citep{chacko2024spectro,ottomano2025nmiracle} fuse infrared with NMR in encoder--decoder or
generative setups~\citep{chacko2024spectro,ottomano2025nmiracle};
Hu \textit{et al.} and NMRTrans invert one-dimensional NMR with multitask
or set-transformer decoders~\citep{hu2024multitask,yang2026nmrtrans};
Alberts and coworkers train a transformer-based architecture on NIST experimental
absorbance and report end-to-end top-$k$ SMILES~\citep{alberts2024ir,alberts2025benchmarks}.
Each of these models is trained on the representation native to its own
corpus, such as a NIST infrared trace or a one-dimensional NMR list, and
emits candidate structures as molecular graphs or SMILES.
Those training inputs are not the heterogeneous printed IR+$^1$H+$^{13}$C
peak lists that IRSpectra-Bench supplies.
Training and scoring on the native format does not make the model a
solver for the lists used here.
Because they are typically scored by a single top-1 or top-$k$ number, a
failure of candidate generation is indistinguishable from a failure of
ranking: both look like a miss.
Only NMRTrans, among these models, is run on the roster below, and only as
an NMR-only baseline: formula and printed $^1$H/$^{13}$C, IR unused
(Appendix~\ref{si:nmrtrans}).
That score is not an IR+$^1$H+$^{13}$C measurement.
A top-$k$ on NIST absorbance or on a simulated trace does not say whether
the true constitution was proposed from a printed three-list experimental
section.

% \subsection{Tool-using elucidation and IR-Agent}
Recent language-model systems occupy three operating regimes, and the
regimes differ in what they can repair.
Tool-using laboratory systems such as ChemCrow~\citep{mbran2024chemcrow}
and Coscientist~\citep{boiko2023coscientist} call instruments, search
tools or reaction planners; they are not instrumented to say whether an
elucidation failure is a missing candidate or a mis-ranked one.
Spectrum-native systems take a spectrum or peak list and re-rank a
\textbf{proposed} pool: tree-search over
MolPuzzle~\citep{zhuang2025treesearch}, SpecMol~\citep{shen2025specmol},
Priessner \textit{et al.}~\citep{priessner2026reasoning},
NMR-Solver~\citep{jin2025nmrsolver} and
NMRAgent~\citep{fang2026nmragent}.
Priessner already notes that a verifier cannot recover a structure that
never entered the pool; we measure that failure on literature peak lists
at $n{=}500$.
SpectraLLM~\citep{su2025spectrallm} is a fine-tuned elucidator trained on
simulated multi-spectral corpora, a different object from printed/reported
experimental peak lists.

IR-Agent~\citep{noh2025iragent} is the closest concurrent infrared
elucidator and the cleanest illustration of why the stage split matters.
It is a tool-using multi-agent system on NIST experimental absorbance: a
translator proposes a SMILES pool, a table-interpretation expert assigns
peaks to functional groups, a retriever expert pulls structurally similar
library spectra, and a structure-elucidation expert re-ranks the
proposed pool.
The architecture is extensible: new chemical information can be
appended to prompts, and it reports top-$k$ SMILES accuracy on a
single-instrument experimental library.
The structure-elucidation expert, like any verifier, can change which
candidate is first; it cannot put a missing constitution into the pool.
Table and retrieval features likewise condition ranking of structures
that were already proposed.
IRSpectra-Bench asks a different measurement on a different object:
literature-reported IR+$^1$H+$^{13}$C peak lists plus molecular formula $f_{\cal M}$, one
out-of-the-box closed-book model, and explicit generation recall versus
verification precision $|$ recall
(Eqs.~\ref{eq:metrics}).
There is no IR-Agent or SpectraLLM row on this roster. This distinction makes the benchmark a diagnostic of where an elucidation pipeline loses the structure, rather than a leaderboard comparison across incompatible inputs. The title therefore emphasizes literature-reported peak lists: the input format determines whether proposal and verification can be separated (see Section~\ref{sec:not-on-roster}).

A third regime compares architectures on a shared suite.
MolQuest~\citep{han2026molquest}, NMRGym~\citep{fang2026nmrgym} and
Espejo Morales \textit{et al.}~\citep{espejo2026agentic}
(education $\to$ industrial: 80.9\% $\to$ 20.6\%) ask which search or
tooling stack wins.
Those suites still need a stage contract if a win is to be attributed
to a better proposer rather than a better ranker.

\vspace{-0.75cm}
\paragraph{Heterogeneous peak lists and CASE}
Most learned suites use simulated spectra or single-instrument libraries
such as NIST infrared or nmrshiftdb2-style
collections~\citep{nist_webbook,kuhn2015nmrshiftdb2,alberts2025benchmarks}.
Literature peak lists are author-transcribed, often incomplete (missing
weak bands or quaternary $^{13}$C), referenced to local solvents and
spectrometers, and stripped of the lineshape and two-dimensional
correlation evidence that CASE engines
exploit~\citep{elyashberg2012case,buevich2016synergistic}.
Matching predicted to observed shifts --- DP4
probability~\citep{smith2010dp4}, HOSE-code
lookup~\citep{bremser1978hose}, CASE
enumeration~\citep{elyashberg2012case} --- is a verification problem on
a generated isomer list.
CASE enumerators have exhaustive recall by construction, subject to the
formula and spectral constraints; published LLM elucidation numbers
rarely state whether the true structure was proposed at all.
SpecX~\citep{xiang2026specx} bounds a related multi-modal spectroscopy
task on simulated traces.

\label{sec:not-on-roster}
NMRTrans~\citep{yang2026nmrtrans} is run here on formula and printed
$^1$H/$^{13}$C lists, with IR unused, because the released model accepts
that printed peak-list interface.
The roster scores are 79/500 top-1 and 102/500 recall, with 9/500 gold
keys in its training split (see Appendix~\ref{si:nmrtrans}).
Those scores are the trained baseline obtained on this interface.
It is not a full IR+$^1$H+$^{13}$C peer. IR-Agent, CASE, Spectro, NMIRacle, the Alberts infrared transformers and
SpectraLLM were not run, because API access, licence, and input do not
match this printed three-list harness.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:benchmark}

IRSpectra-Bench is a frozen roster, the InChIKey-14 contract, and the
three rates in Eq.~\ref{eq:factor} (Appendix~\ref{si:eqs}).
Section~\ref{sec:not-on-roster} is the list of systems this section does not score.

\subsection{Problem setup}
\label{sec:preliminaries}
The molecular elucidation problem provides a molecular formula $f_{\cal M}$ (as from
high-resolution mass spectrometry) together with three author-transcribed
lists: IR band positions $I \subset \mathbb{R}_{+}$ in cm$^{-1}$,
$^1$H multiplets $H$ (shifts, integrals, and couplings as printed), and
$^{13}$C shifts $C \subset \mathbb{R}$ in ppm; see Listing~\ref{lst:payload} for an example.
The target is the covalent constitution $y^\star$ of the unknown,
represented as a SMILES string and scored at the first fourteen characters
of the RDKit InChIKey (Eq.~\ref{eq:ik14}).
Stereo, tautomer and salt-form distinctions that live in the remaining
InChIKey characters are held out of the headline and reported on a
validate-clean subset (Appendix~\ref{si:extras}).
No name, SMILES, scaffold, or retrieved spectrum is part of the input;
the solver never sees the source paper.

A solver returns a ranked pool $\mathcal{C}=(c_1,\ldots,c_k)$ with
$k\leq 3$.
Generation recall, top-1 and self-ranking precision $|$ recall are the
evaluation protocol of this paper, defined for the frozen $n{=}500$
IRSpectra-Bench roster under that candidate budget.
Generation recall is the fraction of the roster for which
$y^\star$ appears anywhere in $\mathcal{C}$.
Top-1 is the fraction for which $c_1$ matches $y^\star$.
Self-ranking precision $|$ recall is the fraction of recalled problems on
which the solver's own order places $y^\star$ first.
An independent forward-verification ranker (Eq.~\ref{eq:chamfer}) may
reorder the same pool; it cannot add a missing constitution.
The $n{=}500$ forward-verify diagnostic then partitions the same pools into
verified, misranked and never-proposed cells
(Appendix~\ref{si:fverify}); that split is not the generation wall.
Because top-1 factorises as recall times precision $|$ recall, a better
ranker cannot raise recall, and the two rates must not be differenced.

\paragraph{Cohort and inputs}
Each of the 500 problems is exactly the textual form reported in
open-access papers: formula plus IR band list plus $^1$H/$^{13}$C shift
lists.
The inputs are author-transcribed IR band and chemical shift lists, not
absorbance traces or FIDs, and therefore a different object from
digitised-spectrum benchmarks~\citep{espejo2026agentic}.
That is also the form a language model reads when it is asked to work
from the literature rather than from an instrument file.

The evaluation set is a frozen roster of 500 question IDs released with
the code.
Problems are drawn from the structure-complete split of \citep{yabbarov2026irexp}
(\texttt{irexp\_resolved}).
The question IDs are a pre-specified list, not an outcome-selected subset
(Appendix~\ref{si:stopping}).
Histograms of molecular weight, ring count, C--F bonds, and nitrogen
atoms for the validate-clean subset ($n{=}295$: locked cohort plus
clean expansion) of \citep{yabbarov2026irexp} data are in Appendix~\ref{si:extras}.
On that plate, the median molecular weight is $306~\mathrm{g{\cdot}mol^{-1}}$ and the median ring
count is two rings.
The thinking-tier expand-500 cut is not in the plate.
Listing~\ref{lst:payload} is the solver-facing contract: formula plus
printed peak lists; gold constitution is held out and scored offline.

\paragraph{Difficulty and scoring}
Difficulty is declared \emph{a priori} by RDKit ring analysis, before any
solver is run: a problem is \emph{simple} if and only if it has at most
two rings, no fused, spiro, or bridgehead system, and at most 22 heavy
atoms; otherwise it is \emph{complex} (248/252 on $n{=}500$).
The released roster is \textbf{near-50/50 by design} (248/252), against an
eligible corpus that is 17.5\% simple / 82.5\% complex.
Table~\ref{tab:headline} therefore puts the balanced-roster top-1 next to
the corpus-reweighted correction on a validate-clean subset.
The reweighted rows are not an $n{=}500$ interval, and they are the number
to quote for an arbitrary literature draw.

A candidate $\hat y$ is correct at constitution \emph{iif} the first
14 characters of the RDKit InChIKey match
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ik14}
s(\hat y, y^\star)
\;=\;
\mathbf{1}\!\big[\mathrm{IK}_{14}(\hat y)=\mathrm{IK}_{14}(y^\star)\big].
\end{equation}
Those 14 characters are the connectivity layer; stereo-sensitive InChIKey
is reported on the validate-clean subset (Appendix~\ref{si:extras}).
External submissions deposit up to three ranked SMILES per \texttt{qid}
(\texttt{docs/LEADERBOARD.md}; \texttt{scripts/score\_submission.py}).
With a ranked pool $\mathcal{C}_i=(c_{i1},\ldots,c_{ik})$, $k\leq 3$,
% \begin{align}
% \tiny
% \label{eq:top1}
% \mathrm{top\text{-}1} \;=\;
% \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n s(c_{i1}, y_i^\star),&\quad \mathrm{prec.}|\mathrm{recall} \;=\;
% \frac{\sum_i s(c_{i1}, y_i^\star)}
% {\sum_i \mathbf{1}[y_i^\star\in\mathcal{C}_i]}\\
% \label{eq:recall}
% \mathrm{recall}@k
% &\;=\;
% \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbf{1}\!\big[\exists\,j\leq k:\; s(c_{ij}, y_i^\star)=1\big].
% \end{align}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:metrics}
\small
\mathrm{top\text{-}1}
= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n s(c_{i1},y_i^\star),
\,
\mathrm{prec.}\mid\mathrm{recall}
= \frac{\sum_i s(c_{i1},y_i^\star)}
       {\sum_i \mathbf{1}[y_i^\star\in\mathcal{C}_i]},
\,
\mathrm{recall}@k
= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
\mathbf{1}\!\left[\exists\,j\le k:
s(c_{ij},y_i^\star)=1\right].
\end{equation}
Top-1 and recall@$k$ share the denominator $n$; precision $|$ recall is
defined only on the recalled subset.
Eq.~\ref{eq:factor} is exactly
$\mathrm{top\text{-}1}=(\mathrm{recall}@k)\times(\mathrm{prec.}|\mathrm{recall})$
when both stages use the same ranked list.
Confidence intervals, where reported, are bootstrap 95\%; paired model
differences use McNemar with Holm correction.
The $n{=}500$ headline does not carry confidence intervals.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}
The experiments run the problem of Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} under a
fixed closed-book harness: one out-of-the-box model family on the frozen
roster, a declared candidate budget, and an optional second ranker that
cannot enlarge the pool.
What follows specifies the payload, the solver, the scoring contract, and
the probes that later sections use to interpret the generation wall.
For further details regarding close-book harness see Section ~\ref{si:closed-book} in the SI. 

\paragraph{Blind payload and solver.}
The solver sees four JSON fields per \texttt{qid}: \texttt{formula} (as from
HRMS), \texttt{ir\_bands\_cm-1}, \texttt{h\_nmr}, \texttt{c\_nmr} --- peak
lists as printed (Listing~\ref{lst:payload}).
No name, SMILES, scaffold, atom-type hint, or retrieved spectrum enters the
prompt; the source paper is not fetched; ground truth is scored offline.
The headline solver is Claude Opus 5 via a consumer subscription,
closed-book, with one sub-agent per batch and an RDKit formula check that
rejects candidates whose elemental composition disagrees with $f$.
The pooled $n{=}500$ roster mixes earlier no-thinking runs with the thinking-tier
expand-500 cut (200 of 500) that completes the cohort.
Both parts use the same InChIKey-14 contract, with no fine-tuning and no paid API.
\textbf{Frozen:} question ids, gold InChIKey-14 keys, prediction JSONL, and the scorers.
Those regenerate 227/500 and 249/500.
\textbf{Not frozen:} model snapshot, temperature, seed, and the batch wrapper;
only the four-field payload was kept.
Early sampler seeds were not stored, so the roster is reusable and not re-drawable
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Scaffold splitting.}
A Bemis--Murcko scaffold split is the appropriate hold-out when a model
is trained on molecules, and generalization is measured on unseen scaffolds.
That design does not apply to the headline solver.
Claude Opus 5 is an out-of-the-box closed-book model; every compound on
the frozen roster is an evaluation item, and the solver is not trained on
a split of IRSpectra-Bench.
Memorisation of a chemical neighbourhood, or of a particular paper, is
examined with the contamination probes: formula only against the full
lists, and accuracy against source-paper year
(Section~\ref{sec:contamination}; Table~\ref{tab:formula-only};
Appendix~\ref{si:year}).
Murcko-scaffold comparisons on the validate-clean subset describe
how close a prediction is to the reference; they are not a data-split
protocol.

\paragraph{Scoring contract and candidate budget.}
A deposit is JSONL of \texttt{\{qid, candidates: [SMILES,\ldots]\}} under a
stated budget.
The scorer reports the three Section~\ref{sec:benchmark} metrics at
InChIKey-connectivity, with bootstrap 95\% CIs on arms that carry them.
Self-ranking and forward-verified re-ranking must be labelled separately:
they share a pool but not a ranker, so their precision $|$ recall figures
are not interchangeable.
Headline protocol: at most three ranked SMILES per \texttt{qid}.
On the 60-compound arm, Claude Opus 5 returned a mean of 2.20 candidates versus 3.00
for the other vendors, so recall rankings across vendors are approximate.
\textbf{Generate-wide} (that arm only) runs ten independent solvers with
up to six SMILES each, then pools and re-ranks; that budget is not the
$n{=}500$ reporting contract.

\paragraph{Forward-verification and probes.}
The second ranker follows generate $\to$ forward-predict $^{13}$C (blind
to the observed list) $\to$ re-rank by symmetric chamfer on $^{13}$C peak
sets $P,Q\subset\mathbb{R}$
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:chamfer}
d(P,Q)
\;=\;
\frac1{|P|}\sum_{p\in P}\min_{q\in Q}|p-q|
\;+\;
\frac1{|Q|}\sum_{q\in Q}\min_{p\in P}|q-p|
\end{equation}
(Section~\ref{sec:forward-verify}).
Chamfer is assignment-free: it remains defined when $|P|\neq|Q|$, which
is the usual case for incomplete printed $^{13}$C lists.
This ranker uses the same candidate pool and therefore cannot raise
generation recall.
Controls, reported in order below, are a formula-only ablation and recency
(Section~\ref{sec:contamination}); four-vendor replication on a
60-compound arm (Section~\ref{sec:cross-vendor}); generate-wide sampling;
HOSE lookup and GNN $^{13}$C verifiers; and a small IRexp-fine-tuned
generator probe that is excluded from headline claims.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
The measurements below are one closed-book harness on the frozen
$n{=}500$ roster, then the 60-compound controls
(Sections~\ref{sec:benchmark}--\ref{sec:setup}).
The generation wall is the lead result.
Forward verification is the secondary drop on the same pools.
Systems named in Section~\ref{sec:not-on-roster} do not appear in these tables.
NMRTrans is scored in Appendix~\ref{si:nmrtrans} as an NMR-only baseline (formula and printed
$^1$H/$^{13}$C; IR unused; 79/500 top-1).
It is not an IR+$^1$H+$^{13}$C row in these tables.

\label{sec:headline}

Table~\ref{tab:headline} summarizes the generation cohort of the closed-book harness. Generation recall is 249/500, and self-ranking selects the correct structure in 227/249 recalled cases, yielding an overall top-1 accuracy of 227/500 (45.4\%).

The resulting generation wall is therefore 227 exact top-1 predictions, 22 candidates that were generated but incorrectly ranked, and 251 structures that were never proposed (Figure~\ref{fig:fig1}). Thus, most failures arise during candidate generation rather than self-ranking. The evaluation roster is approximately balanced between simple and complex molecules (248/252), whereas only 17.5\% of the eligible corpus is simple. Reweighting to the corpus composition therefore reduces top-1 accuracy to 26.5\% [21--32] and recall to 31.3\% [25--37]. Forward verification on the same candidate pools achieves 204/500 top-1, 23 fewer correct predictions than self-ranking (Section~\ref{sec:forward-verify}); this ranking effect is distinct from the generation wall. Performance also depends strongly on molecular complexity: top-1 accuracy is 65\% (161/248) for simple molecules but 26\% (66/252) for complex molecules. Once the molecular formula $f_{\cal M}$is fixed, fused and polycyclic systems admit substantially more constitutions compatible with the reported spectral peak lists. On the validate-clean subset, scaffold-level recovery is 64\% (80\% simple, 48\% complex), with a mean best Tanimoto similarity of 0.66. Accuracy also decreases with molecular size: 67.9\% top-1 for molecules with $\leq 15$ heavy atoms, 42.8\% for 16--25, and 16.7\% above 25 ($n{=}53/152/90$; Appendix~\ref{si:extras}). Among 137 analysable top-1 misses in an instrumented slice, \textbf{76.6\%} are constitutional isomers of the true structure, whereas only \textbf{22.6\%} share its Murcko scaffold. The typical failure therefore preserves composition but not the underlying connectivity, rather than representing a small regiochemical perturbation of the correct structure. A battery-electrolyte subset ($n{=}46$) is similarly recall-bound, with 26\% top-1 accuracy; results from the underpowered four-model Claude ladder are reported in Appendix~\ref{si:ladder24}.

\subsection{Forward verification}
\label{sec:forward-verify}

Forward verification asks whether an independent $^{13}$C ranker can
repair the misses that remain after the solver has proposed a pool.
When the true structure is among the candidates on the 60-compound arm,
the chamfer ranker selects it in $16/19$ cases (84\%).
On this arm the margin over self-ranking (14/60 $\to$ 16/60 top-1) is small and
unresolved; we do not claim an accuracy advance on that arm.
On $n{=}500$, forward-verify top-1 is $204/500$, a drop of 23 from self-rank
$227/500$.
The forward-verify diagnostic --- not the generation wall --- is
$204$ verified / $45$ misranked / $251$ never proposed
(Appendix~\ref{si:fverify}).
No re-ranking of an already-proposed pool repairs the $251$ compounds
where the true structure never entered the list.

\paragraph{Worked cases.}
Three deposits instantiate these modes (Appendix~\ref{si:cases}).
\textbf{main-R06} (C$_{29}$H$_{30}$FNO$_{2}$): true acridinedione never enters the 3-candidate
pool (Tanimoto-0.90 regioisomer); verifier unused.
\textbf{v3-R25} (C$_{10}$H$_{14}$N$_{2}$O): verify-save --- self-rank prefers 3-pyridyl,
forward $^{13}$C recovers 2-pyridyl (chamfer 0.42 vs 1.30~ppm; Figure~\ref{fig:fig-mechanism}).
\textbf{v3-R26} (C$_{14}$H$_{11}$N$_{3}$O$_{3}$): verify-fail --- true 3-nitrophenyl isomer in
the pool but 2-nitro ranked first (chamfer 1.35 vs 1.36~ppm).

\paragraph{Generate-wide.}
If proposal is the expensive stage, enlarging the pool should lift recall
more than swapping the ranker.
Ten solver agents, up to six candidates each, pooled and re-ranked on the
60-compound arm, move recall 32\% $\to$ \textbf{42\%} and top-1 23\%
$\to$ \textbf{30\%} (McNemar $p{=}0.34$); verification precision falls
84\% $\to$ 72\% as noisier candidates enter.
The training-free ceiling remains recall-limited
(the 23\% $\to$ 27\% $\to$ 30\% ladder is Figure~\ref{si:fig:ladder}).

\paragraph{Non-LLM verifiers.}
The same candidate sets can be re-ranked without a language model.
A HOSE-style lookup ties self-ranking; a modest GNN reaches 59/65 (91\%)
versus the LLM verifier's 58/65 (89\%), a directional gap.
Deranging observed $^{13}$C pairings collapses precision ($p{=}0.001$),
so the ranker is using the printed shifts rather than a composition
prior.
A small IRexp-fine-tuned generator pooled with Claude lifts recall on an
instrumented slice $33.5\%\to54.1\%$ and top-1 $28.4\%\to35.1\%$
(McNemar $p{=}0.015$).
The recall limit is therefore a property of \textbf{training-free elicitation}:
once a proposer is allowed to train on the same peak-list resource,
recall moves.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The reusable objects are the frozen $n{=}500$ deposits and the InChIKey-14
scorer.
On those deposits one closed-book harness self-ranks a proposed pool in
$227/249$ cases and fails to propose the true constitution in 251 of 500
(generation wall $227/22/251$; Figure~\ref{fig:fig1}).
Forward verification is the secondary drop, $227/500 \to 204/500$
(Appendix~\ref{si:fverify}).
Table~\ref{tab:headline} places corpus-reweighted 26.5\% beside 45.4\%.
They are the score of one closed-book harness.
They are not a score for IR-Agent or CASE, which remain unscored.
NMRTrans is a separate trained NMR-only baseline on the same roster
(79/500 top-1, IR unused; Appendix~\ref{si:nmrtrans}), not a full multi-modal peer.

\paragraph{Reporting contract.}
External work should deposit ranked SMILES and report (i)~top-1
constitution, (ii)~generation recall
, (iii)~verification precision $|$ recall
(Eqs.~\ref{eq:metrics}), with a stated candidate budget.
Nonsignificant ladder gains (protocol-slice forward-verification vs
self-ranking, $p{=}0.55$; generate-wide top-1, $p{=}0.34$) say where
effort should go.
They are not accuracy advances.
IRexp fine-tuning already lifts recall on an instrumented slice
$33.5\%\to54.1\%$, which is the direction a proposer-side intervention
moves.

\paragraph{Implications for tool-using elucidation.}
\label{sec:agentic}
A second $^{13}$C ranker does not recover the 251 never proposed.
The expensive stage is anything that enlarges
$P(\text{true}\in\text{pool})$: broader sampling, formula-constrained
decode, isomer enumeration, or library search that can introduce a
missing constitution.
IR-Agent-style table-interpretation and retrieval
experts~\citep{noh2025iragent} are feature agents for ranking a pool that
already exists; they inherit the same limit as the chamfer ranker, and
they were not run here.
MolQuest- and Espejo-style architecture
search~\citep{han2026molquest,espejo2026agentic} still needs the stage
contract if a win is to be attributed to proposal rather than re-ranking.
On literature peak lists, the binding improvement is a better proposer.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\label{sec:limitations}
What is frozen, and what is not, is stated in Section~\ref{sec:setup}:
deposits and the InChIKey-14 scorer regenerate the totals; the consumer
subscription has no pinned snapshot, and the $n{=}500$ pool mixes
no-thinking rows with a thinking-tier expand-500 cut (200 of 500).
That mix is not a controlled comparison.
Solver transcripts were audited for zero ground-truth access.
Contamination is bounded by the formula-only ablation ($23\%\to5\%$ on $n{=}60$)
and flat recency ($r{=}{-}0.007$), and is not excluded.
The object is printed band lists with formula supplied as from HRMS.
The unscored systems in Section~\ref{sec:not-on-roster} were not run.
Forward-verify versus self-rank drops by 23 on $n{=}500$ and is unresolved
on the protocol slice ($p{=}0.55$); generate-wide top-1 is unresolved
($p{=}0.34$); the $n{=}24$ ladder is underpowered.
No expert-chemist audit was run.
Leave-one-modality on paired $n{=}60$ was completed:
$^1$H is the only significant leave-one-out ($58.3\%\to35.0\%$, McNemar $p{=}0.001$);
IR and $^{13}$C do not (both $56.7\%$, $p{=}1.000$);
formula$+$IR matches the formula-only floor ($5.0\%$).
Extraction fidelity is inherited from IRexp rather than re-audited here.
The battery subset is literature electrolyte chemistry, not operando cells;
scoring is single-sample; the roster inherits PMC-OA organic bias.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

IRSpectra-Bench is a frozen roster of literature-reported IR, $^1$H, and
$^{13}$C peak lists, read by one closed-book harness and scored with an
InChIKey-14 contract.
Each problem is the textual form of an experimental section: a molecular
formula together with author-transcribed band positions and shift tables.
The solver does not see the source paper, and the gold constitution is
scored offline.
The fourteen-character key fixes the covalent constitution that counts as
correct, so the same scorer can replay a later deposit.
The roster, the contract, and the deposits are a diagnosis of this
harness.

On that harness, the failure that binds is generation recall.
Top-1 factorises into two stages with different denominators: whether the
true constitution enters the proposed pool, and whether, given that it
did, the ranker places it first.
A ranker cannot insert a structure that never entered the pool.
Forward verification of a finished pool is a ranking diagnostic in the
same sense.
It can change which proposed structure is placed first.
It does not restore a constitution that was never proposed.
Generation on the balanced roster is 45.4\% top-1, and most of what that
figure misses was never proposed.
The change that would move the headline is a better proposer.

The balanced roster and the corpus reweighting are separate scores.
The roster is balanced simple against complex by design, and on that
roster the harness scores 45.4\% top-1.
Reweighted to the eligible corpus, top-1 on a validate-clean subset is
26.5\%, the figure for an arbitrary draw from the literature.
The first is the score of the frozen roster.
The second is the score of the same harness under the corpus mix.
A reported top-1 has to say which of the two was computed.

The deposits and the InChIKey-14 scorer are the diagnostic for follow-on
work.
Under that contract, a true constitution that never enters the pool is a
proposer failure, and one that enters but is not placed first is a ranker
failure.
A later deposit can be read as one or the other without a new scorer.
We release both.
The claim stops at this harness.
