\begin{abstract}
Recovering a covalent constitution from spectra is the first inverse problem
a synthetic chemist meets after a reaction.
Open-access papers record that evidence as \textbf{literature-reported}
IR, $^1$H and $^{13}$C NMR \textbf{peak lists}: printed wavenumbers and
shift tables, not digitised absorbance traces or FIDs.
Frontier models report high recovery on curated, single-library, or
simulated spectra, yet it remains unclear whether an off-the-shelf model,
given only a molecular formula and those printed lists, can recover the
correct constitution, and which stage of an evaluate-then-rank pipeline
fails when it cannot.

We release \textbf{IRSpectra-Bench}, a blind peak-list benchmark of
\textbf{$n{=}500$} compounds with a fixed RDKit InChIKey-connectivity
contract and decomposable \textbf{generation recall} and
\textbf{verification precision} metrics.
A frontier model recovers \textbf{45.4\%} top-1 (227/500) and \textbf{49.8\%}
generation recall (249/500).
When the true structure is proposed, self-ranking places it first in
$227/249$ cases (91.2\%).
Independent forward verification of the same pools yields the $n{=}500$ wall of
\textbf{204} verified / \textbf{45} misranked / \textbf{251} never proposed:
proposal is the expensive stage (\textbf{propose $\gg$ verify}).
The same recall $\ll$ precision asymmetry replicates across
\textbf{four vendor families}.
Formula-only controls collapse; accuracy is flat in source-paper year;
broader generation sampling lifts recall, whereas verification alone barely
moves top-1.
From published top-$k$ figures, reduced recall accounts for
\textbf{68--83\%} of the accuracy collapse from curated or simulated spectra
to real heterogeneous peak lists.
Frozen predictions, scorers and code are released for mechanical re-evaluation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Recovering a covalent constitution from spectra is the routine inverse problem
of synthetic and analytical chemistry.
After a reaction, the first measurements a bench chemist records are a
mid-infrared spectrum and one-dimensional $^1$H and $^{13}$C NMR.
Infrared band positions encode functional groups and conjugation:
carbonyl stretches, O--H and N--H envelopes, aromatic C=C, and the paired
nitro bands that survive even a terse experimental section.
NMR chemical shifts, multiplicities and integrals encode the number and
neighbourhood of hydrogen and carbon environments.
The form that enters the literature is almost never the instrument file.
Experimental sections print \textbf{peak lists}: wavenumbers in cm$^{-1}$ and
shift tables in ppm, often with coupling constants, and only rarely with
intensities or lineshape.
Two-dimensional correlations (COSY, HSQC, HMBC) that classical
computer-assisted structure elucidation (CASE) uses to constrain
connectivity~\citep{elyashberg2012case,elyashberg2015case} are frequently
absent from those tables.
A model that reads the open literature must therefore invert constitution
from formula plus three printed lists, not from a digitised trace or a
full CASE constraint set.

Learned systems now consume those same reports as sequence or tool-using
tasks.
Encoder--decoder models train on paired spectra--structure
corpora~\citep{chacko2024spectro,ottomano2025nmiracle,alberts2024ir};
puzzle benches and search systems report high recovery on curated or
education spectra~\citep{kamber2026chemist,guo2024molpuzzle,zhuang2025treesearch,espejo2026agentic}.
Reported recovery for the same model can invert with the harness
(GPT-4o, 1.4\% vs.\ 57.8\% on MolPuzzle~\citep{guo2024molpuzzle,zhuang2025treesearch}),
which already shows that a single end-to-end number is not a stable
property of the chemistry.
The operational setting that matches how chemists read papers is stricter:
an off-the-shelf model, a molecular formula (as from high-resolution mass
spectrometry), IR/$^1$H/$^{13}$C lists as printed, a fixed
molecular-representation scoring contract, and a measurement of
\textbf{which stage binds} when recovery fails.
That stage is either proposal of the true constitution or ranking among
candidates that were already proposed.

Any system that returns a ranked candidate list factorises as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:factor}
\text{top-1} \;=\; \underbrace{P(\text{true} \in \text{pool})}_{\text{generation recall}}
\times
\underbrace{P(\text{rank}=1 \mid \text{true} \in \text{pool})}_{\text{verification precision}}.
\end{equation}
The same split is the classical CASE architecture: generate constitutional
isomers, then match predicted to observed
spectra~\citep{elyashberg2015case,priessner2026reasoning}.
Recall is a fraction of $n$ problems; precision $|$ recall is a fraction of
the recalled subset.
The two rates have different denominators and must not be differenced
(Eqs.~\ref{eq:top1}--\ref{eq:prec}).
A better ranker cannot raise recall, because re-ranking cannot recover a
structure that never entered the
pool~\citep{priessner2026reasoning}.
We report both factors on a frozen literature-peak-list roster at
$n{=}500$, so that a high top-1 cannot hide a missing-candidate failure
and a high verifier score cannot be read as a solved elucidator.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{IRSpectra-Bench} --- a blind, complexity-stratified peak-list benchmark ($n{=}500$)
        with a pre-registered InChIKey-connectivity scorer and community reporting contract
        (top-1, generation recall, verification precision $|$ recall).
  \item \textbf{A recall-bound diagnosis} on a frontier LLM: propose $\gg$ verify.

  \item \textbf{A literature decomposition} that recovers the same split from published top-$k$

\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Trained spectra $\to$ structure models}
Paired corpora have produced strong in-distribution elucidators, and they
set the default reporting language for the field.
Spectro and NMIRacle fuse infrared with NMR in encoder--decoder or
generative setups~\citep{chacko2024spectro,ottomano2025nmiracle};
Hu \textit{et al.}\ and NMRTrans invert one-dimensional NMR with multitask
or set-transformer decoders~\citep{hu2024multitask,yang2026nmrtrans};
Alberts and coworkers train infrared transformers on NIST experimental
absorbance and report end-to-end top-$k$ SMILES~\citep{alberts2024ir,alberts2025benchmarks}.
Each of these systems learns a joint map from spectral tokens to graphs
or strings.
Because they are typically scored by a single top-1 or top-$k$ number, a
failure of candidate generation is indistinguishable from a failure of
ranking: both look like a miss.
The released IRSpectra-Bench scorer is the intended comparison route for
Spectro, NMIRacle, Alberts IR transformers and CASE
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Tool-using elucidation and IR-Agent}
Recent language-model systems occupy three operating regimes, and the
regimes differ in what they can repair.
Tool-using laboratory systems such as ChemCrow~\citep{mbran2024chemcrow}
and Coscientist~\citep{boiko2023coscientist} call instruments, search
tools or reaction planners; they are not instrumented to say whether an
elucidation failure is a missing candidate or a mis-ranked one.
Spectrum-native systems take a spectrum or peak list and re-rank a
\textbf{proposed} pool: tree-search over
MolPuzzle~\citep{zhuang2025treesearch}, SpecMol~\citep{shen2025specmol},
Priessner \textit{et al.}~\citep{priessner2026reasoning},
NMR-Solver~\citep{jin2025nmrsolver} and
NMRAgent~\citep{fang2026nmragent}.
Priessner already notes that a verifier cannot recover a structure that
never entered the pool; we measure that failure on literature peak lists
at $n{=}500$.
SpectraLLM~\citep{su2025spectrallm} is a fine-tuned elucidator trained on
simulated multi-spectral corpora, a different object from printed
experimental lists.

IR-Agent~\citep{noh2025iragent} is the closest concurrent infrared
elucidator and the cleanest illustration of why the stage split matters.
It is a tool-using multi-agent system on NIST experimental absorbance: a
translator proposes a SMILES pool, a table-interpretation expert assigns
peaks to functional groups, a retriever expert pulls structurally similar
library spectra, and a structure-elucidation expert re-ranks the
proposed pool.
The architecture is extensible: new chemical information can be
appended to prompts, and it reports top-$k$ SMILES accuracy on a
single-instrument experimental library.
The structure-elucidation expert, like any verifier, can change which
candidate is first; it cannot put a missing constitution into the pool.
Table and retrieval features likewise condition ranking of structures
that were already proposed.
IRSpectra-Bench asks the complementary measurement on a different
object: literature-reported IR+$^1$H+$^{13}$C peak lists plus formula,
an off-the-shelf closed-book model, and explicit generation recall
versus verification precision $|$ recall
(Eqs.~\ref{eq:top1}--\ref{eq:prec}).
On-bench scores for IR-Agent and SpectraLLM are left to the released
scorer.

A third regime compares architectures on a shared suite.
MolQuest~\citep{han2026molquest}, NMRGym~\citep{fang2026nmrgym} and
Espejo Morales \textit{et al.}~\citep{espejo2026agentic}
(education $\to$ industrial: 80.9\% $\to$ 20.6\%) ask which search or
tooling stack wins.
Those suites still need a stage contract if a win is to be attributed
to a better proposer rather than a better ranker.

\subsection{Heterogeneous peak lists and CASE}
Most learned suites use simulated traces or single-instrument libraries
such as NIST infrared or nmrshiftdb2-style
collections~\citep{nist_webbook,kuhn2015nmrshiftdb2,alberts2025benchmarks}.
Literature peak lists are author-transcribed, often incomplete (missing
weak bands or quaternary $^{13}$C), referenced to local solvents and
spectrometers, and stripped of the lineshape and two-dimensional
correlation evidence that CASE engines
exploit~\citep{elyashberg2012case,buevich2016synergistic}.
Matching predicted to observed shifts --- DP4
probability~\citep{smith2010dp4}, HOSE-code
lookup~\citep{bremser1978hose}, CASE
enumeration~\citep{elyashberg2012case} --- is a verification problem on
a generated isomer list.
CASE enumerators have exhaustive recall by construction, subject to the
formula and spectral constraints; published LLM elucidation numbers
rarely state whether the true structure was proposed at all.
SpecX~\citep{xiang2026specx} bounds a related multi-modal spectroscopy
task on simulated traces.
IRSpectra-Bench is an openly redistributable literature-peak-list suite
with an explicit stage decomposition, measured on Claude and replicated
across three other model families
(Section~\ref{sec:contamination}).

\section{IRSpectra-Bench}
\label{sec:benchmark}

IRSpectra-Bench is the evaluation object of this paper: a frozen roster of
literature peak-list problems, a representation contract, and three rates
that make the factorisation in Eq.~\ref{eq:factor} measurable.
This section formalises the inverse problem, then describes the cohort
and the scorer that later sections run against.

\subsection{Problem setup}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

An elucidation problem supplies a molecular formula $f$ (as from
high-resolution mass spectrometry) together with three author-transcribed
lists: infrared band positions $I \subset \mathbb{R}_{+}$ in cm$^{-1}$,
$^1$H multiplets $H$ (shifts, integrals and couplings as printed), and
$^{13}$C shifts $C \subset \mathbb{R}$ in ppm.
The target is the covalent constitution $y^\star$ of the unknown,
represented as a SMILES string and scored at the first fourteen characters
of the RDKit InChIKey (Eq.~\ref{eq:ik14}).
Stereo, tautomer and salt-form distinctions that live in the remaining
InChIKey characters are held out of the headline and reported on a
validate-clean subset in the SI.
No name, SMILES, scaffold or retrieved spectrum is part of the input;
the solver never sees the source paper.

A solver returns a ranked pool $\mathcal{C}=(c_1,\ldots,c_k)$ with
$k\leq 3$.
Three rates are reported.
Generation recall is the fraction of the $n{=}500$ roster for which
$y^\star$ appears anywhere in $\mathcal{C}$.
Top-1 is the fraction for which $c_1$ matches $y^\star$.
Self-ranking precision $|$ recall is the fraction of recalled problems on
which the solver's own order places $y^\star$ first.
An independent forward-verification ranker (Eq.~\ref{eq:chamfer}) may
reorder the same pool; it cannot add a missing constitution.
The $n{=}500$ forward-verify wall then partitions the roster into
verified, misranked and never-proposed cells
(Figure~\ref{fig:fig-wall}).
Because top-1 factorises as recall times precision $|$ recall, a better
ranker cannot raise recall, and the two rates must not be differenced.

\subsection{Cohort and inputs}

Each of the 500 problems is exactly the textual form reported in
open-access papers: formula plus IR band list plus $^1$H/$^{13}$C shift
lists.
The inputs are author-transcribed \textbf{band and shift lists}, not
absorbance traces or FIDs, and therefore a different object from
digitised-spectrum benchmarks~\citep{espejo2026agentic}.
That is also the form a language model reads when it is asked to work
from the literature rather than from an instrument file.

The evaluation set is a frozen roster of 500 question ids released with
the code.
Problems are drawn from the structure-complete split of IRexp
(\texttt{irexp\_resolved})~\citep{yabbarov2026irexp}.
The cohort has median molecular weight 306 and median two rings
(histograms of MW, ring count, C--F bonds and nitrogen atoms are in
the SI).
Listing~\ref{lst:payload} is the solver-facing contract: formula plus
printed peak lists; gold constitution is held out and scored offline.

\subsection{Difficulty and scoring}

Difficulty is declared \emph{a priori} by RDKit ring analysis, before any
solver is run: a problem is \emph{simple} if and only if it has at most
two rings, no fused, spiro or bridgehead system, and at most 22 heavy
atoms; otherwise it is \emph{complex} (248/252 on $n{=}500$).
The released benchmark is \textbf{near-50/50 by design}.
The eligible corpus is 17.5\% simple / 82.5\% complex, so the leaderboard
is not a draw from the corpus mix.
Reweighted to corpus composition on a validate-clean subset, top-1 falls
to \textbf{26.5\%} [21--32] and recall to \textbf{31.3\%} [25--37]
(SI; not an $n{=}500$ figure).
Headline scores in Table~\ref{tab:headline} are the 50/50 leaderboard;
the reweighted figures remain the better estimate for an arbitrary
literature draw.

A candidate $\hat y$ is correct at constitution if and only if the first
14 characters of the RDKit InChIKey match
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ik14}
s(\hat y, y^\star)
\;=\;
\mathbf{1}\!\big[\mathrm{IK}_{14}(\hat y)=\mathrm{IK}_{14}(y^\star)\big].
\end{equation}
Those 14 characters are the connectivity layer; stereo-sensitive InChIKey
is reported on the validate-clean subset (SI).
External submissions deposit up to three ranked SMILES per \texttt{qid}
(\texttt{docs/LEADERBOARD.md}; \texttt{scripts/score\_submission.py}).
With a ranked pool $\mathcal{C}_i=(c_{i1},\ldots,c_{ik})$, $k\leq 3$,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:top1}
\mathrm{top\text{-}1}
&\;=\;
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n s(c_{i1}, y_i^\star),\\
\label{eq:recall}
\mathrm{recall}@k
&\;=\;
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbf{1}\!\big[\exists\,j\leq k:\; s(c_{ij}, y_i^\star)=1\big],\\
\label{eq:prec}
\mathrm{prec.}|\mathrm{recall}
&\;=\;
\frac{\sum_i s(c_{i1}, y_i^\star)}
{\sum_i \mathbf{1}[y_i^\star\in\mathcal{C}_i]}.
\end{align}
Top-1 and recall@$k$ share the denominator $n$; precision $|$ recall is
defined only on the recalled subset.
Eq.~\ref{eq:factor} is exactly
$\mathrm{top\text{-}1}=(\mathrm{recall}@k)\times(\mathrm{prec.}|\mathrm{recall})$
when both stages use the same ranked list.
Confidence intervals, where reported, are bootstrap 95\%; paired model
differences use McNemar with Holm correction.
The $n{=}500$ headline does not carry confidence intervals.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

The experiments run the problem of Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} under a
fixed closed-book harness: one off-the-shelf model family on the frozen
roster, a declared candidate budget, and an optional second ranker that
cannot enlarge the pool.
What follows specifies the payload, the solver, the scoring contract, and
the probes that later sections use to interpret the wall.

\paragraph{Blind payload and solver.}
The solver sees four JSON fields per \texttt{qid}: \texttt{formula} (as from
HRMS), \texttt{ir\_bands\_cm-1}, \texttt{h\_nmr}, \texttt{c\_nmr} --- peak
lists as printed (Listing~\ref{lst:payload}).
No name, SMILES, scaffold, atom-type hint or retrieved spectrum enters the
prompt; the source paper is not fetched; ground truth is scored offline.
The headline solver is Claude Opus via a consumer subscription,
closed-book, with one sub-agent per batch and an RDKit formula check that
rejects candidates whose elemental composition disagrees with $f$.
The core protocol uses no fine-tuning, no paid API, and \textbf{no thinking
tier}.
Inference is \textbf{not} exactly reproducible (no pinned snapshot,
temperature or seed; Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
Scoring is reproducible: frozen predictions, ground truth and scorers
regenerate every training-free number.

\paragraph{Scoring contract and candidate budget.}
A deposit is JSONL of \texttt{\{qid, candidates: [SMILES,\ldots]\}} under a
stated budget.
The scorer reports the three Section~\ref{sec:benchmark} metrics at
InChIKey-connectivity, with bootstrap 95\% CIs on arms that carry them.
Self-ranking and forward-verified re-ranking must be labelled separately:
they share a pool but not a ranker, so their precision $|$ recall figures
are not interchangeable.
Headline protocol: at most three ranked SMILES per \texttt{qid}.
On the 60-compound arm Claude returned mean 2.20 candidates versus 3.00
for the other vendors, so recall rankings across vendors are approximate.
\textbf{Generate-wide} (that arm only) runs ten independent solvers with
up to six SMILES each, then pools and re-ranks; that budget is not the
$n{=}500$ reporting contract.

\paragraph{Forward-verification and probes.}
The second ranker follows generate $\to$ forward-predict $^{13}$C (blind
to the observed list) $\to$ re-rank by symmetric chamfer on $^{13}$C peak
sets $P,Q\subset\mathbb{R}$
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:chamfer}
d(P,Q)
\;=\;
\frac1{|P|}\sum_{p\in P}\min_{q\in Q}|p-q|
\;+\;
\frac1{|Q|}\sum_{q\in Q}\min_{p\in P}|q-p|
\end{equation}
(Section~\ref{sec:forward-verify}).
Chamfer is assignment-free: it remains defined when $|P|\neq|Q|$, which
is the usual case for incomplete printed $^{13}$C lists.
This ranker uses the same candidate pool and therefore cannot raise
generation recall.
Controls, reported in order below, are formula-only and recency
(Section~\ref{sec:contamination}); four-vendor replication on a
60-compound arm (Section~\ref{sec:cross-vendor}); generate-wide sampling;
HOSE lookup and GNN $^{13}$C verifiers; and a small IRexp-fine-tuned
generator probe that is excluded from headline claims.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The measurements below follow the protocol of
Sections~\ref{sec:benchmark}--\ref{sec:setup} on the frozen $n{=}500$
roster and on the 60-compound control arm.
We first report headline top-1 and generation recall, then the
formula-only and recency bounds, the four-vendor replication, the
forward-verification ladder, and the literature decomposition.
The same qualitative split (recall below precision $|$ recall, with
most of the residual mass in never-proposed) appears in every cut
that we can score.

\quad simple ($n{=}248$) & 65\% (161/248) \\
\quad complex ($n{=}252$) & 26\% (66/252) \\
generation recall (top-3) & \textbf{49.8\%} (249/500) \\

The simple/complex split in Table~\ref{tab:headline} is the expected
isomer-space effect: once the formula is fixed, fused and polycyclic
systems admit many more constitutions compatible with the same printed
lists, and top-1 falls from 65\% (161/248) to 26\% (66/252).
On the validate-clean subset, scaffold-level recovery is 64\% (simple
80\%, complex 48\%) and mean best Tanimoto is 0.66; accuracy falls with
size (67.9\% top-1 at $\leq$15 heavy atoms, 42.8\% at 16--25, 16.7\%
above 25; $n{=}53/152/90$; SI).
On an instrumented slice, of analysable top-1 misses, \textbf{76.6\%}
are constitutional isomers of the true structure; only \textbf{22.6\%}
share the true Murcko scaffold.
The typical miss is therefore wrong connectivity at the right
composition, not a near-miss of regiochemistry.
A battery-electrolyte subset ($n{=}46$) is recall-bound (26\% top-1);
the underpowered four-model Claude ladder is SI.
Self-ranking precision $|$ recall on $n{=}500$ is $227/249$ (91.2\%),
the generation-side complement of the 45.4\% top-1.
Forward-verification of the same pools is reported separately in
Section~\ref{sec:forward-verify} and Figure~\ref{fig:fig-wall}; the two
precision figures are not interchangeable.

\subsection{Forward verification}
\label{sec:forward-verify}

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.64\linewidth]{fig_mechanism.pdf}
  \caption{Case v3-R25 shows self-rank preferring 3-pyridyl, while forward
  $^{13}$C recovers 2-pyridyl (chamfer 0.42 vs 1.30~ppm; Table~\ref{tab:cases}).}
  \label{fig:fig-mechanism}
\end{figure}

Forward verification asks whether an independent $^{13}$C ranker can
repair the misses that remain after the solver has proposed a pool.
When the true structure is among the candidates on the 60-compound arm,
the chamfer ranker selects it in $16/19$ cases (84\%).
The margin over self-ranking (14/60 $\to$ 16/60 top-1) is small and
unresolved; it diagnoses the same cheap-verification regime rather than
an accuracy advance.
On $n{=}500$, self-ranking precision $|$ recall remains $227/249$
(91.2\%).
The $n{=}500$ forward-verify wall is $204$ verified / $45$ misranked /
$251$ never proposed (Figure~\ref{fig:fig-wall}).
No re-ranking of an already-proposed pool repairs the $251$ compounds
where the true structure never entered the list.

\paragraph{Worked cases.}
Three deposits instantiate the wall (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:cases}).
\textbf{main-R06} (C$_{29}$H$_{30}$FNO$_{2}$): true acridinedione never enters the 3-candidate
pool (Tanimoto-0.90 regioisomer); verifier unused.
\textbf{v3-R25} (C$_{10}$H$_{14}$N$_{2}$O): verify-save --- self-rank prefers 3-pyridyl,
forward $^{13}$C recovers 2-pyridyl (chamfer 0.42 vs 1.30~ppm; Figure~\ref{fig:fig-mechanism}).
\textbf{v3-R26} (C$_{14}$H$_{11}$N$_{3}$O$_{3}$): verify-fail --- true 3-nitrophenyl isomer in
the pool but 2-nitro ranked first (chamfer 1.35 vs 1.36~ppm).

\paragraph{Generate-wide.}
If proposal is the expensive stage, enlarging the pool should lift recall
more than swapping the ranker.
Ten solver agents, up to six candidates each, pooled and re-ranked on the
60-compound arm, move recall 32\% $\to$ \textbf{42\%} and top-1 23\%
$\to$ \textbf{30\%} (McNemar $p{=}0.34$); verification precision falls
84\% $\to$ 72\% as noisier candidates enter.
The training-free ceiling remains recall-limited
(the 23\% $\to$ 27\% $\to$ 30\% ladder is reprinted in the SI).

\paragraph{Non-LLM verifiers.}
The same candidate sets can be re-ranked without a language model.
A HOSE-style lookup ties self-ranking; a modest GNN reaches 59/65 (91\%)
versus the LLM verifier's 58/65 (89\%), a directional gap.
Deranging observed $^{13}$C pairings collapses precision ($p{=}0.001$),
so the ranker is using the printed shifts rather than a composition
prior.
A small IRexp-fine-tuned generator pooled with Claude lifts recall on an
instrumented slice $33.5\%\to54.1\%$ and top-1 $28.4\%\to35.1\%$
(McNemar $p{=}0.015$).
The wall is therefore a property of \textbf{training-free elicitation}:
once a proposer is allowed to train on the same peak-list resource,
recall moves.

\subsection{Literature comparison}
\label{sec:literature}

The same factorisation can be read off published top-1 and top-$k$
figures without re-running those systems.
Any report of top-1 $= a$ and top-$k = b$ implies recall $\geq b$ and
conditional precision $\leq a/b$.
Applying that identity to published numbers
(\texttt{scripts/literature\_decomposition.py}) recovers the same
pattern: recall carries most of the drop when the data become real and
heterogeneous.
The full row set (this work, NMR-Solver, NMRAgent, Espejo, Alberts,
SpecX) is in the SI.

\textbf{Three groups changed only the data}, and recall carried \textbf{68\%} (NMR-Solver
simulated $\to$ real~\citep{jin2025nmrsolver}), \textbf{70\%} (SpecX random $\to$ scaffold~\citep{xiang2026specx}) and \textbf{83\%}
(Espejo education $\to$ industrial~\citep{espejo2026agentic}) of each collapse.
Recall binds where spectra are real and heterogeneous; ranking dominates on simulated or
single-library data.
The solver-alone precision column on $n{=}500$ is self-ranking (227/249); the
forward-verify row is an instrumented protocol slice (SI).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

On the frozen $n{=}500$ roster a frontier model is a reliable ranker of
an already-proposed pool and an unreliable proposer of the true
constitution (Figure~\ref{fig:fig-wall}).
The contribution is that measurement: the benchmark and the stage
decomposition on literature peak lists as printed.
The numbers do not license a solved elucidator.

\paragraph{Reporting contract.}
External work should deposit ranked SMILES and report (i)~top-1
constitution (Eq.~\ref{eq:top1}), (ii)~generation recall
(Eq.~\ref{eq:recall}), (iii)~verification precision $|$ recall
(Eq.~\ref{eq:prec}), with bootstrap CIs and a stated candidate budget.
Nonsignificant ladder gains (forward-verification vs self-ranking,
$p{=}0.55$; generate-wide top-1, $p{=}0.34$) are diagnostic of where
effort should go, not accuracy advances to quote as method wins.
Corpus-reweighted top-1 (\textbf{26.5\%} on a validate-clean subset)
remains the better estimate for an arbitrary literature draw; it was
not re-scored as $n{=}500$.
IRexp fine-tuning already lifts recall on an instrumented slice
$33.5\%\to54.1\%$, which is the direction a proposer-side intervention
should move.

\paragraph{Implications for tool-using elucidation.}
\label{sec:agentic}
Once the true constitution is in the pool, verification is cheap:
self-ranking usually selects it, and a second $^{13}$C ranker
does not change the qualitative picture.
The expensive stage is therefore anything that enlarges
$P(\text{true}\in\text{pool})$: broader sampling, formula-constrained
decode, isomer enumeration, or library search that can introduce a
missing constitution.
IR-Agent-style table-interpretation and retrieval
experts~\citep{noh2025iragent} are complementary \emph{feature} agents
for ranking a proposed pool; they inherit the same limit as our
chamfer ranker.
MolQuest- and Espejo-style architecture
search~\citep{han2026molquest,espejo2026agentic} still needs this stage
contract if a win is to be attributed to proposal rather than
re-ranking.
The binding improvement on literature peak lists is a better proposer.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\label{sec:limitations}
Scoring is mechanical; solver runs were transcript-audited for zero
ground-truth access.
The headline harness is Claude Opus via a consumer subscription, with
no pinned snapshot, temperature, seed or thinking tier: inference is
not bit-exact, scoring of frozen deposits is, and batch instruction
text was not captured.
Higher-reasoning inference modes are not treated as a scored method
result.
Contamination is bounded by the formula-only drop ($23\%\to5\%$) and
flat recency, and is not excluded.
The object is band lists rather than traces; the formula is supplied
as from HRMS; stereo top-1 on a validate-clean subset is in the SI.
Forward-verify versus self-rank is unresolved ($p{=}0.55$), as is
generate-wide top-1 ($p{=}0.34$); the $n{=}24$ four-model arm is
underpowered.
The ladder diagnoses a bottleneck.
SpectraLLM, IR-Agent, Spectro, NMIRacle, Alberts IR transformers and
CASE are not scored on this bench; the released scorer is the
comparison route.
Protocol-slice and expand-500 chamfer arms remain SI, not a second wall.
An expert-chemist audit was not run; a leave-one-modality arm was
abandoned; extraction fidelity is a property of IRexp and the released
filters, and the parser is not re-audited here.
The battery subset is literature electrolyte chemistry, not operando
cells; scoring is single-sample; the roster inherits PMC-OA organic
bias.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

IRSpectra-Bench makes structure elucidation from literature peak lists
measurable as a two-stage inverse problem.
On the generation cohort ($n{=}500$), a frontier model recovers 45.4\%
top-1 (227/500) and 49.8\% generation recall (249/500); corpus-reweighted
top-1 on a validate-clean subset is 26.5\%.
Proposal is the expensive stage: the forward-verify wall is $204$
verified / $45$ misranked / $251$ never proposed.
The same recall $\ll$ precision split replicates across four vendor
families and is recovered from published top-$k$ figures, where reduced
recall accounts for 68--83\% of the collapse onto real heterogeneous
lists.
We release frozen predictions and a mechanical scorer so that later
systems can report the same three numbers on the IRexp-derived roster.
